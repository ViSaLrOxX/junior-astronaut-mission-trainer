Sources: real vs simulated

Real NASA facts used in this game

  • MOXIE, on NASA's Perseverance rover, really made oxygen from Mars's carbon dioxide air. The O2 Generator is named after that experiment.
  • The International Space Station is protected by Earth's magnetic field. A Moon or Mars base is not, which is why shielding matters in this game.
  • Apollo 13's crew really came home safe in 1970 after an explosion, by using their lander as a lifeboat.
  • NASA's Artemis program targets the lunar south pole because shadowed craters there hold water ice.

Real recorded space-weather events

  • June 2018: a planet-wide dust storm on Mars cut sunlight so much that NASA's solar-powered Opportunity rover never woke up again. The storm in the Mars story is modelled on that event.
  • August 1972: a huge solar particle storm erupted between the Apollo 16 and 17 missions. The solar particle event in the Moon story is modelled on that kind of storm.
  • 13 March 1989: a geomagnetic storm knocked out Hydro-Québec's power grid in about 90 seconds; six million people lost power for roughly nine hours. Sources: NASA Goddard Space Flight Center and Hydro-Québec.
  • September 1859: the Carrington Event, the strongest geomagnetic storm on record, set telegraph systems sparking.

Simulated for learning

Every number in the mission - oxygen, water, food, power, parts, health, morale, budgets and dates - is a simplified teaching model, not real telemetry. Mission lengths and costs are not NASA figures.
